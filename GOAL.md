# The Goal

We're building a great blue heron that lives in a small framed screen — standing knee-deep in still water, watching, coiling, striking at fish, preening, flying out and coming back. It's not a video loop: its motion is *generated* by a tiny model on a cheap chip, entirely offline, the way a candle's flame can be computed rather than recorded.

To make it true to life we measured the real bird first — neck mechanics off its actual skeleton, strike timings and wingbeat off dozens of filmed herons — then modeled the neck as a 20-link mechanism, and we're drawing it in the manner of Audubon's *Birds of America*.

It ends as a small ambient object for a shelf: a heron in a box, alive when you plug it in.
