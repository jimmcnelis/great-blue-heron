Repaint the first image — a monochrome pose study of a great blue heron — as a finished hand-colored engraving in the manner of Audubon's Birds of America. The bird must be EXACTLY the bird in the second and third images, which are the approved finished drawings of this same heron: identical slate-blue plumage and engraved hatch character, white head, black brow sweeping into black occipital plumes, yellow-orange dagger bill, golden eye, shaggy breast plume hanging in separate strands, rusty-brown thighs of the same short length and muted color as in the third image, two thin dark legs, warm ivory plate paper, a shallow waterline at the feet. The black shoulder patch must stay SMALL — the size it is in the third image — never a large black saddle over the back. GEOMETRY LOCK: keep the pose, position, size and silhouette of the bird in the first image exactly as they are; do not recompose the scene, do not raise or spread the wings, do not change the neck's angle or length. If the bill tip touches the water, draw the disturbance as fine engraved ripple rings around the bill tip in the same plate manner; if the bill is leaving the water, a few fine engraved drip lines. No text, no border, no signature, no fish, no other animals, no scenery beyond the waterline.
