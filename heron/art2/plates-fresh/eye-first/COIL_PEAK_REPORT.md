## COIL — ranking (by resemblance to a real coiled heron)

1. **c7 → coil_1** — The most *loaded* bird of the eight: head lowest, bill
   vertical, eye fixed down the bill at the water; the S is the tightest of the
   full-body plates and the plume is a lush curtain. It reads as a bird a
   half-second from striking. Costs: eye sits low against its computed anchor
   (diagnostics), neck's descending pass still doesn't touch the ascending one.
2. **c2 → coil_2** — Lushest strand curtain of the batch and a tight S with a
   compact head; the fold's two passes come closest to lying against each
   other here. Costs: thighs heavily scale-textured (the alert winner's known
   cost, repeated); the eye reads slightly ringed.
3. **c1 → coil_3** — Clean, level-backed, family-true; head low and watching.
   Costs: the front of the neck runs slate-dark (less pale than the
   photographs), and the S is a touch more open than c7/c2.
4. **c6** — Soft, elegant, finer hand; the S is graceful but less loaded.
5. **c3** — Full plume, but the eye is the most ringed/staring of the batch
   and the S hangs open.
6. **c8** — A good bird (tight S, lush plume) carrying a printed caption —
   "ARDEA HERODIAS. --- GREAT BLUE HERON." — along the bottom. The family
   plates carry no text; the caption is the cost.
7. **c4** — The fold-depth lever produced a rounder, domed back instead; the
   S is no deeper than the base candidates'.
8. **c5** — The freest candidate is the least coiled: head highest, back most
   domed — it reads as the alert-crouch variant (design §4.1's noted variant),
   not the fishing coil.

**The shared coil cost, named plainly:** no candidate achieves the design's
fold *as drawn structure* — layered feather compression with the passes lying
against each other across an ink seam. All eight store the neck in an open,
graceful S-curve. The chord numbers (below) land near the design band because
the head hangs low and close, but the arc rides in a tall loop, not in
compressed layers. The photographs won this argument too: UR t1 and qpYk are
themselves moderate coils, and only the 57Y portrait — a head-and-neck
close-up — holds the truly deep fold.

## PEAK — ranking (by resemblance to a real standing spear)

1. **c6 → peak_1** — The straightest spear of the eight: shoulder, neck, skull
   and bill run in nearly one line, the neck slim at extension, feet planted
   together, rings exactly at the bill tip. Costs: body pitch is mild (the
   bird is fairly level), and the hand is the softest/palest of the batch.
2. **c2 → peak_2** — Tail cocked up, body pitched forward, spear fairly
   straight with one shallow dip; legs clean and planted, rings at the tip.
   The most *action-true* of the three finalists.
3. **c4 → peak_3** — The lushest trailing plume and a long reach, head on the
   spear's line at the end. Costs: a visible mid-neck dip (the throat sags
   into a shallow U before the head), body fairly level.
4. **c8** — Near-twin of c4 with slightly less reach; same throat dip.
5. **c3** — Contact rings drawn largest (three bold concentric rings — the
   most emphatic water mark of the batch); legs stiff and straight-on, neck
   dip pronounced.
6. **c1** — Fine spear and plume, but the legs are anatomically wrong: an
   extra low bend in each shin and feet splayed flat like a wader on mud —
   the only candidate whose legs fail the eye.
7. **c7** — The body-pitch lever overshot: feet stride far apart, so the bird
   reads as stepping/lunging rather than planted — the lunge flavour the
   design defers to its own sheet.
8. **c5** — The photograph-led candidate erected the plume into a fountain
   spray above the neck — a display crest, not a strike; plus a deep S-dip
   left in the neck.

**The shared peak cost, named plainly:** none of the eight unfolds to the
measured spear. Diagnostics put the finalists' chord_base at 0.31–0.43 against
the design's 0.62–0.71 — the birds reach down-forward to about half to
two-thirds of the mechanism's extension, heads at mid-frame, and every neck
keeps a residual throat dip. This is the design doc's rule-4 wall (the
generator's heron prior excludes the true spear), softened by photographic
conditioning but not broken by it. What the batch does prove: the *look* —
family hand, planted feet, rings at the tip — survives the strike pose; the
*reach* does not, yet.
