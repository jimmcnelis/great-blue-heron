## pier-heron-night-01.mp4 (uploaded by Jimmy 2026-11-02)

- Source: UniFi Protect pier/dock camera, night, infrared. 1280×720,
  30 fps, 59.6 s. Original upload:
  workspace/user/media_library/video/ce/ce1921...mp4
- Contents: fly-in + landing on the dock, a slow patrol walk the length
  of the dock, a front→profile turn, long statue stands at the dock
  edge. NO strike/catch in this clip.

### Measured (Mordecai, frame sampling 2026-11-02)

- **Landing:** bird first visible ~1.2 s (low glide over the water,
  wings spread); flare begins ~1.7 s — wings raised high, legs dangling
  as thin trailing lines; touchdown ~2.2 s at the dock edge; two
  wing-fold beats; fully settled statue by ~3.5 s. Total event ≈ 2.3 s
  from appearance to still. Chart check: landing chart = 120 frames
  (5.0 s @24) — the real bird is faster; the flare+fold structure matches.
- **Turn (front → profile, ~90°):** bird stands FRONT-ON to camera
  35–37 s; head begins rotating first (~37.2 s); body follows through
  3/4; full profile + walking by ~39.5–40 s. Head lead ≈ 0.5–1.0 s;
  total reorientation ≈ 2.3–2.8 s. Chart check: the 72-frame (3.0 s)
  canonical turn for 180° is consistent with this 90° measurement.
  BONUS: the front-on and 3/4 frames are genuine orientation reference
  for the v1.2 rig's yaw stops — narrow body, neck centred, legs close.
- **Stillness:** 42.7–58 s the bird stands at the dock edge essentially
  motionless (15+ s; only micro head movements) — a measured floor for
  idle_hunt holds.
- **Walk (from 1 s sampling):** deliberate steps with freezes between,
  head steady; crosses the dock over ~25 s. Step-cycle detail to be
  measured if v1.2 needs it.

## kayak-heron-day-02.mp4 (uploaded 2026-11-02)

- Blue-hour COLOR, 720p30, 42.3 s. The heron stands on Jimmy's kayak
  (Old Town Sportsman) moored at the dock.
- It walks the kayak stern→bow in careful, deliberate steps (~0–20 s) —
  foot placement on an unstable platform, body adjusting pitch as it
  goes; then hunts from the bow: upright alert (~26–31 s), then a deep

  crisp. The rig/model sheet should carry the plume mass.

## kayak-strike-night-06.mp4 (uploaded 2026-11-02) — THE STRIKE

- Night IR, kayak camera, 17.1 s. A complete catch-and-eat from the
  kayak bow. This is a PLUNGE strike from a perch, not the wading
  neck-spear — the whole body launches.
- Measured phases (10 fps sampling, Mordecai):
  - Aim: statue on the bow tip, neck extended forward-down, ≥6 s
    before launch (clip opens mid-aim).
  - Launch ~7.5 s: wings mantle up, body pitches forward off the
    perch; head-first water entry ~8.3 s. Launch→entry ≈ 0.6–0.8 s.
  - Grappling: head/neck submerged at the surface ~8.3–9.3 s (≈1 s).
  - Emerges standing in the shallows beside the kayak 9.3–10.0 s;
    wing-assisted scramble back onto the bow by ~12.6 s; wings spread
    in recovery 12.6–13.2 s.
  - **TOSS-FLIP at ~13.5 s:** a small bright elongated object is
    airborne just left of the bill for ~0.2 s and re-caught — the
    study's toss-flip phase, confirmed on our bird. [Measured, high
    confidence, single observation]
  - Swallow ~13.8–14.4 s (head work; the neck bulge's travel is NOT
    resolvable at this distance — stays canonical); statue again by
    ~15 s. Catch→settled total ≈ 7 s.
  - The dip phase is n/a here (the bird was already in the water).

## water-takeoff-night-07.mp4 (uploaded 2026-11-02) — THE TAKEOFF

- Night IR, water camera, 16.6 s. The bird is IN the water (floating
  low like a goose, neck folded — it swims) and takes off from the
  surface. Measured at 12 fps:
  - Floats/paddles ~6 s (head dips at the surface early on).
  - Gather: head rises, body lifts in the water (~7.5–7.9 s).
  - First wingbeat ~7.9 s; neck STRETCHES STRAIGHT OUT during the
    launch (the study's takeoff detail, confirmed); feet clear the
    surface ≈ 0.7–0.9 s after the first beat; 2–3 deep beats climb it
    out of frame, legs trailing drips.
  - INK REFERENCE: the departure leaves a churned bright patch + an
    expanding ring that stays visible 7+ s — the exact water signature
    for takeoff in the ink layer.
