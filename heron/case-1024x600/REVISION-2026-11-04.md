**Why:** Jimmy unboxed the Waveshare ESP32-P4-WIFI6-Touch-LCD-7B (Rev1.1)
and found the physical board doesn't match the shipped case's assumptions —
above all, **all the USB lives on one short edge**, not the bottom edge the

## What changed, and the measured source for each change

1. **USB-side relief window in the bezel tray** — the tray wall on the
   frame's LEFT side (= the board's USB edge; back-view right maps to
   front-view left) is opened from frame-y 28 to 117, from the slab
   back through the tray back, ~0.8 mm past the pocket wall line.
   The front slab is NOT cut — the frame's face and display border
   are unchanged.
   - *Why:* clears the whole edge stack (ON/OFF switch, USB-OTG
     Type-A, Type-C "USB", Type-C "USB TO UART") so the panel drops
     in and lifts out vertically with **zero connector contact**
     (Jimmy's damage concern is the design constraint), admits the
     power plug from the side, and leaves the ON/OFF switch
     reachable with the case closed.
   - *Source:* port positions from the official outline drawing
     (Waveshare product page, "outline dimensions"): chained dims
     from the board's bottom edge — USB-TO-UART centre 13.57 mm,
     USB-C centre +15.28 = 28.85 mm, USB-A shell ≈14 mm span above
     that, ON/OFF switch 10.68 mm from the top edge. Mapped into
     frame coordinates the stack needs frame-y ≈ 31–115; the window
     spans 28–117 (margin at both ends, absorbing the ±1.1 mm
     PCB-to-panel centring uncertainty). Photos (Jimmy's, 2026-11-04)
     confirm the stack order and that the switch actuator and
     connector faces sit at/past the laminate edge.
2. **JST-side relief window** — same treatment on the frame's RIGHT
   wall, frame-y 36–114.
   - *Why:* the I2C/UART/CAN/RS485 PH2.0 housings protrude past the
     laminate on that edge (visible in Jimmy's board photo) and the
     RESET/BOOT buttons sit in the top corner; with only ~0.74 mm
     nominal side slack they would scrape the wall the same way.
     This window is clearance insurance — no plug access needed.
3. **Back cover: matching notch + speaker grille deleted.**
   - The cover's left edge is notched over the same frame-y 28–117
     span, cut through the cover *and* its locating rim. The rim
     normally intrudes 0.7 mm into the pocket around its whole
     perimeter — right where the USB-A socket (the tallest edge
     part, ~7.0–7.6 mm above the PCB) stands. The notch removes
     that interference and keeps plugs/switch reachable with the
     cover on.
   - The speaker grille slots are gone: the kit speaker is an
     **enclosed dual-driver box** on a PH2.0 lead (Jimmy's photo),
     not the loose driver the grille was cut for, and it does not
     fit inside the closed frame (see reconciliation item 4). For
     the prototype it lives **outside the frame on its lead**.
     No fake internal home was forced.
4. **Mounting-hole pitch corrected: 155 × 88 → 156 × 89 mm**
   (`hole_pitch_x/y`). The README's PLACEHOLDER is now VERIFIED
   from the official outline drawing (pitch dim
...[truncated 5310 chars]ensions
   156.00 × 89.00 on the 164.00 × 97.00 envelope). Affects the back
   cover's locating nubs and `hole_plate` (print it, lay it on the
   board, confirm). The design still doesn't depend on the nubs.
5. **Mic chin holes OFF** (`mic_holes = false`). Jimmy's photo +
   the official port map put MIC1/MIC2 at the PCB's bottom corners
   on the **back face** (|x| ≈ 74 mm from centre, behind the
   panel's bottom edge, facing backward). Front chin holes at
   ±58 mm could never have served them. The chin is solid —
   the README's own drill-later fallback — and a final version
   would port the back cover at the corners instead.
6. **Stack height: unchanged** (`stack_t = 13`). Worst-point
   estimate from class-typical component heights: glass → USB-A
   top ≈ 12.9 mm < the 13.8 mm pocket, and the only point where
   the cover could have met the USB-A is removed by the notch.
   Bumping `stack_t` on an unverified number would have changed
   the tray depth and broken the dock slot fit, so it stays;
   `fit_corner` + one ruler read settle it for the final version.

**Unchanged, deliberately:** frame externals 183.48 × 131.37 ×
18.4 mm; the display window and front face; the chin raceway and
bottom notch (the final version's hidden cable route — a future
left-wall raceway can drop the side-entered cable into it without
moving the dock's exit); radar window, BH1750 ledge, vents, screw
pattern; the whole dock (Jimmy's ruling).

## Validation of the regenerated meshes

- All six STLs are **watertight** (pure-Python edge-pairing check:
  0 edges not shared by exactly 2 triangles, every part).
- Bounding boxes: bezel 183.36 × 131.31 × 16.20 mm; back 183.36 ×
  131.31 × 2.90; dock_lower 196 × 76 × 13.70; dock_upper 196 × 76
  × 23.40; hole_plate 180 × 112.71 × 1.60; fit_corner 79.88 ×
  79.97 × 16.20 — all matching the design dimensions.
- **Windows proven open:** a z = 9 mm cross-section of the bezel
  mesh shows 0 mesh crossings through either side-wall band inside
  the window spans (the shipped bezel shows 4 per band — its walls
  were solid there), while the wall below the USB window (chin
  band) still crosses — the window is a window, not a missing wall.
- **Dock proven untouched:** no dock parameter or line of dock
  code changed, and dock_lower renders with identical triangle
  count, volume and bounding box from the pre- and post-revision
  source (688 tris, 64.8 cm³). The CGAL "may not be 2-manifold"
  export warning on dock_lower also occurs with the pre-revision
  source — pre-existing, and the mesh itself passes watertight.

## Verified vs still assumed

**Verified (official sources):** PCB envelope 164.00 × 97.00;
hole pitch 156 × 89; panel 164.28 × 99.17 and its margins; the
right-edge port chain (13.57 / 28.85 / switch 10.68 from top);
port identities and functions (docs "What's On Board" #14–16:
#15 Type-C "USB" = power supply + programming + debugging; #16
USB-TO-UART also power/debug; #14 USB-OTG Type-A host).
**Verified (Jimmy's photos + hands-on):** board identity Rev1.1;
CAMERA socket empty (no camera — the no-camera rule holds); JST
housings protrude past the left laminate edge; MIC1/MIC2 at the
back-face bottom corners; speaker is an enclosed dual-driver box
with four mounting ears on a PH2.0 lead; the tray fit/catch
behaviour that motivated the windows.
**Still assumed (would need a ruler):** total stack thickness
(glass front → tallest back point; the panel-module thickness is
not published anywhere); speaker box L×W×H including ears; the
PCB centred on the panel within ±1.1 mm (windows carry margin
for it; `hole_plate` + `fit_corner` confirm on the real board);
USB-A shell height 7.0–7.6 mm (industry standard for the part).

## Print settings recap (from the case README)

- **Tolerances in these files are the PETG-final values**
  (`fit_clear = 0.6`). Jimmy's plan is PETG; a PLA prototype from
  the same files fits ~0.2–0.3 mm looser per side — the safe
  direction for a fit check (README "PLA prototype vs PETG final").
- Layer 0.2 mm; walls 4–5; infill 20% gyroid on the dock,
  15–20% elsewhere.
- Support-free orientations: bezel front face DOWN (pocket up);
  back cover outer face DOWN (rim up); dock bottom DOWN.
- Hardware unchanged: 4 × M3×8 into brass heat-set inserts
  (Ø4.3 pilots) for the back cover.

## Assembly deltas for the prototype

- The board can go in/out of the tray freely — the windows exist
  so you never lever it against the connectors. Set the ON/OFF
  switch ON before or after closing; it's reachable through the
  window either way.
- Power: plug the USB-C into the **"USB"** Type-C (the middle
  Type-C on the board's right edge) from outside the frame's left
  side. (The lower Type-C, "USB TO UART", also powers/debugs.)
- Speaker: leave it external on its lead for now. The TF card, if
  ever wanted, goes in before the cover — its slot faces the
  board's bottom edge inside the case.
